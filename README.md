# ClarityCare Suicide Analytics – Power BI Dashboard

## Overview
This Power BI project analyses global suicide incidence across age groups, sex, generations, and countries.  
The dashboard provides a comprehensive view of demographic risk patterns and highlights key influencers that contribute to increases in suicide numbers.  
The goal is to support data-driven public health insights and inform prevention strategies.

## Key Insights
- **Total suicide incidence:** 6.75M across 101 countries.
- **Highest-risk generation:** Boomers, accounting for 35.14% of total suicides.
- **Sex differences:** Males recorded 5.19M suicides compared to 1.56M among females.
- **Age group trends:** Highest incidence among individuals aged **35–54 years** (1.95M).
- **Key influencers:**  
  - Being **male** increases suicide numbers by ~254.  
  - Being in the **Boomer generation** increases suicide numbers by ~222.

## Features of the Dashboard
- **Global Overview:** Displays total number of countries (101) and total suicide incidence (6.75 M) for quick context.
- **Generational Analysis:** Highlights suicide incidence by generation — Boomers record the highest numbers, followed by Silent and Generation X.
- **Age Group Trends:** Visualises suicide incidence across age brackets, showing the 35–54 year group as the most affected.
- **Sex and Age Breakdown:** Compares male and female suicide counts across age groups, revealing a strong male predominance.
- **Key Influencer Analysis:** Uses Power BI’s AI visual to identify factors that most influence suicide numbers — sex and generation are the top drivers.
- **Interactive Filters:** Allows users to slice data by sex, generation, and age group for deeper exploration.
- **Data‑Driven Insights:** Supports public‑health decision‑making by uncovering demographic patterns and high‑risk segments.


## How to Use This Dashboard
- **Filter by sex and generation:** Use the slicers to focus on specific groups (e.g. male Boomers, female Millennials).
- **Explore age patterns:** Hover over the age group charts to see exact suicide counts and compare risk across age bands.
- **Drill into countries:** Use the country table to identify where suicide incidence is highest and how it relates to GDP per capita.
- **Interpret key influencers:** Review the Key Influencers visual to understand which factors (sex, generation, age) most strongly drive increases in suicide numbers.
- **Combine filters:** Apply multiple filters together (e.g. sex + generation + age group) to explore high‑risk segments and refine insights.

## Recommendation
Based on the demographic and generational patterns identified in this analysis, the following actions are recommended to support targeted suicide‑prevention strategies:

1. **Prioritise interventions for high‑risk generations**  
Boomers and the Silent Generation account for the largest share of suicide incidence. Tailored mental‑health programmes, community outreach, and age‑specific support services should be prioritised for these groups.

2. **Strengthen male‑focused prevention strategies**  
Males represent the majority of suicide cases across all age groups. Expanding gender‑sensitive interventions — including stigma reduction, early screening, and accessible support channels — is essential.

3. **Target the 35–54 age group with focused support**  
This age bracket shows the highest incidence. Workplace mental‑health initiatives, financial‑stress counselling, and mid‑life crisis support programmes may help reduce risk.

4. **Enhance early‑intervention programmes for younger populations**  
Although incidence is lower among individuals aged 5–24, early prevention is critical. Schools, universities, and youth‑focused organisations should be equipped with proactive mental‑health resources.

5. **Use influencer insights to guide resource allocation**  
Key Influencer analysis shows that sex and generation strongly predict suicide incidence. Prevention strategies should be designed with these demographic drivers in mind to maximise impact.

6. **Support country‑level policy development**  
Countries with higher incidence may benefit from strengthened national suicide‑prevention frameworks, improved access to mental‑health services, and targeted public‑health campaigns.

7. **Promote continuous monitoring and data‑driven decision‑making**  
Regularly updating and reviewing suicide data will help identify emerging trends, evaluate intervention effectiveness, and support evidence‑based policy adjustments.


## Conclusion
This Power BI report provides a detailed and interactive view of global suicide patterns, highlighting demographic and generational disparities.  
By identifying key influencers and high-risk groups, the dashboard supports public health professionals, policymakers, and researchers in understanding suicide trends and designing targeted prevention strategies.
